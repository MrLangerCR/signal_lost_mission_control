LEVEL 2 / 5 — DATA & BINARY

DECODE

Computers store every letter as a number, and every number as binary (1s and 0s). Kepler-9 sent its status as four binary bytes. Use the reference chart to convert each byte to a letter, then type the word it spells.

Binary place values from left to right are 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Add up the place values wherever there's a 1. That total is the letter's position code — match it on the chart.
MISSION LOG — REQUIRED BEFORE LAUNCH

If just one bit in one byte had flipped, what would have happened to the decoded word?

LEVEL 3 / 5 — COMPUTING SYSTEMS

DIAGNOSE

Kepler-9's camera isn't sending images. Every computing system is a chain of hardware and software parts working together — when one breaks, the whole chain stops. Follow the diagnostic questions to find the real cause.
Diagnosis works from the outside in: check power first, then the connection between parts, then the part itself. Ruling things out is how you find the real cause.
MISSION LOG — REQUIRED BEFORE LAUNCH

How did ruling out possibilities help you land on the real cause?

LEVEL 4 / 5 — DIGITAL CITIZENSHIP

TRANSMIT

A partner space agency in another country is asking mission control to send them Kepler-9's raw sensor data right now, before it's been checked for errors. What should your team do?
Good digital citizenship isn't about refusing to share — it's about sharing responsibly: verify data, give credit, and communicate clearly about what's known versus unconfirmed.

LEVEL 5 / 5 — NETWORKS

ROUTE

Data doesn't travel in a straight line — it hops from node to node until it reaches its destination, avoiding failed connections along the way. Click connected nodes to route the packet from SRC to DST, avoiding blocked (red) nodes. You can only move up, down, left, or right.
There's more than one working path. If you get stuck, back up a step (click the last node you placed again to remove it) rather than starting completely over.
